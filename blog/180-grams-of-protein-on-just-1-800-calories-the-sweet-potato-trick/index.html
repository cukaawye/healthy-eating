180 Grams of Protein on Just 1,800 Calories. The Sweet Potato Trick That Makes Dieting Feel Like

Article by Julien Raby

Last updated on

Eating high‑protein, low‑calorie meals doesn’t have to mean forcing down tasteless chicken and broccoli day after day.

Fitness influencer Ail Suede recently shared exactly how he gets 180 grams of protein while keeping his intake under 1,800 calories, and his dishes actually look tasty.

  • Save

For anyone aiming to shed fat but keep muscle, this ratio hits the sweet spot: about 10 grams of protein for every 100 calories consumed.

In his step‑by‑step guide, Suede shows that this way of eating is not only doable, but also satisfying, filling, and surprisingly inventive.

Why a High‑Protein, Low‑Calorie Approach Is Effective

Merging a high protein diet with a calorie deficit creates ideal conditions for body recomposition; protein helps protect lean muscle while boosting fullness, making it easier to stay within a lower‑calorie range.

Suede points out that knowing what to eat is crucial. Without a clear plan, reaching protein goals on a low‑calorie budget can become frustratingly hard.

The Basics: Choosing Intelligent Protein and Carbohydrate Sources

His strategy focuses on picking foods that give the most volume and nutrients for the fewest calories. Protein choices include whole eggs, ground chicken, chicken breast, protein powder, and reduced‑fat peanut butter.

For carbs, he opts for gold potatoes, sweet potatoes, bananas, oat milk, and various vegetables, all selected for their filling properties and nutrient density.

You’re going to find that this diet is full of healthy, wholesome meals that will keep you full while keeping your calories low and protein high.

Meal One: The Most Satisfying 512‑Calorie Dish You’ll Ever Have

Suede describes his first meal as possibly the most satisfying 512‑calorie plate you could imagine, featuring 200 grams of potatoes cooked together with five whole eggs, delivering massive volume and staying power.

Eggs supply complete protein and satiating fats, while potatoes are among the most filling carbohydrate sources; the sheer amount of food makes 512 calories feel like a banquet rather than a restriction.

Look at how much food you’re getting for 500 calories here, guys.

He adds flavor without extra calories by cooking the eggs sunny‑side up and using the yolk as a dip for crispy potatoes; the potatoes are diced, briefly microwaved, then finished in a pan with cooking spray for perfect crispness.

Meal Two: Ground Chicken Meatballs Paired with Sweet Potato Mash

Ground chicken provides an ultra‑lean, protein‑rich base for Suede’s pre‑workout meal, offering nearly 25 grams of protein per serving with very little fat and calories.

Instead of serving plain sweet potato, Suede turns this nutritious carb into a special side: he mashes cooked sweet potato with a splash of oat or almond milk, a packet of zero‑calorie sweetener, and a pinch of salt.

The result is a delicious, lightly sweet accompaniment that adds almost no calories while greatly enhancing flavor; paired with ground chicken meatballs and a California vegetable blend, the second meal supplies roughly 200 grams of sweet potato and double the protein portions.

This literally looks like health. Look at how delicious this looks, guys.

Meal Three: Post‑Workout Protein Smoothie

About 30 minutes after his workout, Suede grabs his go‑to post‑exercise drink: a thick, satisfying protein smoothie that totals just 270 calories.

Protein smoothies play several roles in a high‑protein, low‑calorie diet.

  • Curb sweet cravings: Naturally satisfies desire for dessert-like foods
  • High volume: Creates substantial fullness despite modest calorie count
  • Convenient protein delivery: Easy way to hit daily protein targets

His blend mixes one cup of unsweetened oat milk, a frozen banana, roughly a cup of ice cubes, and a scoop of vanilla ice‑cream flavored whey protein, resulting in a luxuriously thick shake that feels indulgent yet stays on target nutritionally.

We are fans of the thick squad here at the YouTube channel.

Meal Four: Chicken Breast Served with Sweet Potato and Peanut Butter

For his fourth meal, Suede serves eight ounces of cooked, boneless, skinless chicken breast topped with two tablespoons of sugar‑free barbecue sauce that adds only 10 calories, alongside 200 grams of sweet potato.

He then adds an unexpected but highly effective twist: a tablespoon of reduced‑fat peanut butter placed in the middle of the warm sweet potato.

As the peanut butter melts, it creates a savory‑sweet taste that curbs cravings while keeping calories low, and the healthy fats help keep you full well into the next morning’s first meal.

Don’t judge a book by its cover. Just let it sit in and it’s like savory and sweet, peanut buttery. It’s delicious.

Analyzing the Figures

All together, Suede’s daily plan adds up to 1,836 calories with a carefully calculated macronutrient split:

  • Protein: 180 grams
  • Carbohydrates: 171 grams
  • Fat: 49 grams

This distribution hits the key target of roughly 10 grams of protein for every 100 calories consumed, a ratio that defines true high‑protein eating and sets the stage for fast fat loss while protecting muscle.

Thoughtful Food Choices Simplify the Process

From Suede’s method, a few clear guidelines can be applied right away:

Prioritize protein density. Chicken breast, ground chicken, eggs, and quality protein powder give you the most protein with the fewest calories, making them the backbone of each meal.

Choose filling carbohydrates. Both regular and sweet potatoes deliver impressive fullness for their calorie count, and studies consistently rank potatoes among the most satiating foods.

Maximize food volume. Generous servings of vegetables, hearty potato portions, and thick smoothies provide both mental and physical satisfaction, making calorie restriction more doable.

Add flavor creatively. Sugar‑free sauces, zero‑calorie sweeteners, and small, flavorful additions like peanut butter turn basic ingredients into truly enjoyable dishes.

Ensuring High‑Protein Eating Is Sustainable Over Time

Suede points out that this strategy has succeeded not only for him but also for thousands of clients he’s guided, with the secret being sustainability—crafting meals that are satisfying enough to stick with over time.

Diets that feel miserable never last. By emphasizing volume, flavor variety, and smart food choices, high‑protein eating becomes something to anticipate rather than endure.

For anyone finding it hard to shed fat while keeping muscle, Suede’s system offers a realistic roadmap: simple cooking, everyday ingredients, and effective results without needing extreme willpower or constant hunger.

Eating plenty of protein while keeping calories low doesn’t have to be a dull, tasteless experience. By choosing the right foods and getting a bit inventive in the kitchen, you can enjoy meals that also help you reach your body composition objectives.
